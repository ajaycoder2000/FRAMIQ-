Timing wheat harvest around a wet forecast

The hardest harvest call is rarely about the grain. It is about the weather window closing behind it.

Wheat harvest timing is a trade-off between two costs: harvesting too wet means drying charges, and harvesting too late means weather risk on standing grain. A forecast changes which of those costs you should accept.

What rain actually does to standing wheat

  • Re-wetting delays harvest — grain has to dry back down, and a wet crop can cost you days you did not budget.
  • Repeated wet-dry cycles are worse than one soaking. Each cycle degrades grain quality and can push test weight down.
  • Pre-harvest sprouting — the serious one. Warm, wet, humid conditions on mature grain can start germination in the head, which collapses falling number and can move a milling sample to feed grade.
  • Lodging — heavy rain with wind on a mature stand flattens it. A lodged crop harvests slower, with higher losses.

The calculation

Harvesting a couple of points above your target moisture and drying is a known, priced cost. Leaving a mature crop standing through an uncertain wet stretch is an unknown cost that ranges from nothing to a grade change.

When the forecast shows a substantial wet period arriving and your grain is close to ready, the early-and-dry option is usually the cheaper risk — particularly if you have drying capacity or the buyer accepts higher moisture with a deduction.

The forecast trigger

More than about 40 mm (1.5 in) forecast across the coming week, on mature or near-mature wheat, is the point to seriously price out harvesting early and drying rather than waiting.

Read the whole window, not the next day

The question at harvest is never "will it rain tomorrow?" It is "how many consecutive dry days do I have, and when does the next one come?"

A single dry day between two wet ones is often not usable — the crop won't have dried down enough to make the most of it. Three consecutive dry days with wind is a genuine harvest window. Wind matters more than most people credit here: it dries the standing crop far faster than still, humid sunshine.

Order your fields by risk, not convenience

When a window is short, sequence matters. Prioritise the fields most exposed to what's coming: varieties prone to sprouting, stands already leaning, blocks that are furthest along, and low-lying ground that will be too soft to travel once it's wet.

The field that is easiest to reach is rarely the field that most needs harvesting first.

Verify before you commit

Every threshold above is a starting point, not a substitute for a moisture meter and your own eyes in the field. Varieties differ in sprouting susceptibility, buyers differ in what they'll accept, and drying costs vary enormously. The forecast tells you how much time you have — it doesn't tell you whether the grain is ready.
